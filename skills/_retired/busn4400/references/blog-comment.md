# BUSN 4400 — Blog Comment Reference

## What This Produces
A single blog comment in Jacob's voice. No sign-off. No "great post."

## Pattern (extracted from Jacob's actual comments)
- Open by engaging directly with the most interesting or underexplored idea in the post — not with praise
- Push that idea further or complicate it — add a layer the author didn't
- Bring in a related concept, analogy, or mechanism from outside the post
- End with a genuine question that opens the next layer of thinking
- 4-6 sentences total, no filler, no sign-off

## Tone
Analytical, direct, confident. Reads like someone who thought carefully about the post and has something real to add. Never sycophantic. Never summarizes what the author already said.

This was a super interesting read, Ian. As I was reading, the privacy thing popped into my head, so you addressing that was great. More generally, this is such a great use of AI. It helped you build a repeatable and easy-to-understand output, and the output only actually uses the Claude API in a severe fallback situation. I also really enjoyed the Danny Ocean name – not enough people know how good Ocean's 11 really is!

**Example 2 (on AI music / study conditioning post):**
Hi Theresa, I liked this blog post a lot, and it was very relatable. My go to is just a "sorry," but I know I need to work on my conversational concentration. What was interesting here, though, was how it wasn't just in conversation, but rather when you were simply listening to music. It was very surprising that you had accidentally been listening to AI-generated music without knowing. On one hand, that raises some ethical questions. But, back to your topic, it's interesting how having music for certain tasks can increase productivity. It's the same idea of how you shouldn't do homework in bed to try and keep rest and work as separate. I am curious if you have noticed that you are starting to recondition with that AI playlist. Also, I wonder if AI could be used to help make playlists to help not only with attention and enjoyment, but also to find new music.

**Example 3 (on Anthropic system prompt leak post):**
Hi Austin, prompt engineering is absolutely the next form of programming. While the big LLMs will have to stay general, I am interested to see how localized models will hardcode logic to reduce repetition and potential ambiguity with prompts. What the leak actually shows is that this is already happening, just at the scaffolding layer rather than the model layer. The permissions system, tool definitions, and command structure are all ways of having constrained behavior around a general model, which produced something closer to a specialized system without retraining anything. The question is whether this approach scales better than fine-tuned local models. Or, potentially the two frameworks converge from opposite ends. I do think the end goal of all of this is a high-level general AI, but I think we are years away from that.
